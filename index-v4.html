A Hybrid Semantic-AI Framework Integrating Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models for Citizen-Centric Digital Government Services in Malaysia

An answer a citizen can trust must be correct against the governing rule, traceable to its source, and accountable when it is wrong. No deployed AI in Malaysian digital government holds all three at once. This is the space between them, made architecture.

Hamim Sairi
Faculty of Computer Science and Information Technology, Universiti Putra Malaysia
Supervisor — Dr. Novia Admodisastro
一 · Introduction

The layer inherits the fragmentation it sits on

Malaysian digital government promises citizen-centricity — MyDIGITAL, AI-RMAP, NAIAP 2026–2030, MD2030 all commit to it — yet services remain organised around agencies: LHDN, SOCSO, JPN, JPJ operate as separate systems behind a common portal. The AI layered on top is predominantly chatbots that retrieve text but cannot verify it.

Malaysia has reached the fourth stage of digital government without resolving the third. The cost lands hardest on the B40 population, which depends most on correct answers and can least absorb a wrong one.

二 · Literature review

Three capabilities. No system on record holds all three.

Capability 1
Computable eligibility
Computed against a policy rule (SHACL over profile triples), not retrieved as pre-written text (Konstantinidis et al., 2025).
Capability 2
Proactive delivery
"No-Stop Government": a service offered before the citizen asks.
Capability 3
Cross-agency chaining
Once-Only: one agency's document is valid evidence for another's process (Karunarathne et al., 2022).
SHACL 與 PROV-O — never combined before

SHACL asks whether an output conforms. PROV-O asks where it came from. Recorded together, each validation becomes a traceable act — the chain the citizen can follow back.

source regulation→ LLM extraction→ committed triple→ SHACL validation→ citizen determination
Malaysia's Data Sharing Act 2025 (Act 864): §13 permissible purpose · §15 refusal grounds → SHACL abstention shape · §21 duty to report → a query over the provenance store.

三 · Methodology

Six activities, in dependency order, across eight semesters

Design Science Research Methodology. Each objective produces and evaluates an artefact in sequence — none answered by observing an existing system (Peffers et al., 2007).

act 1Problem identification — complete.
act 2Define objectives — complete.
act 3Design and development of A1 → A2 → A3.
act 4Demonstration — a proof of concept, two life events.
act 5Evaluation — scenarios, baselines, expert validation.
act 6Communication — dissertation, three articles, viva.
