A Hybrid Semantic-AI Framework Integrating Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models for Citizen-Centric Digital Government Services in Malaysia

An answer a citizen can trust must be correct against the governing rule, traceable to its source, and accountable when it is wrong. No deployed AI in Malaysian digital government provides all three. builds the architecture that does.

Exhibit 01 / Introduction

The layer inherits the fragmentation it sits on

Malaysian digital government promises citizen-centricity — MyDIGITAL (2021), AI-RMAP (2021), NAIAP 2026–2030, and MD2030 all commit to it — yet services remain organised around agencies: LHDN, SOCSO, JPN, and JPJ operate as separate systems behind a common portal. The AI layered on top is predominantly chatbots that retrieve text but cannot verify it.

FINDING

Malaysia has reached the fourth stage of digital government (AI on top) without resolving the third (integration underneath) — the AI inherits the fragmentation instead of correcting it. The cost lands hardest on the B40 population, which depends most on correct social-protection answers and can least absorb a wrong one (Devisakti et al., 2023).

§1.2 · Three structural gaps, three artefacts

General-purpose LLMs hallucinate on 58–88% of verifiable legal questions; even purpose-built retrieval tools err on 17–33% (Dahl et al., 2024; Magesh et al., 2025).

Exhibit 02 / Literature review

Three capabilities. No system on record has all three.

Six themes, 2021 onward — citizen-centric government, semantic technologies, KG–LLM integration, governance as machine-enforced constraint, hybrid systems already built, and the Malaysian context — consolidated into five gaps and a conceptual framework.

Capability 1
Computable eligibility
Eligibility computed against a policy rule (SHACL over profile triples), not retrieved as pre-written text (Konstantinidis et al., 2025).
Capability 2
Proactive delivery
"No-Stop Government": a service offered before the citizen asks — mattering most to those who don't know it exists.
Capability 3
Cross-agency chaining
Once-Only: one agency's issued document serves as valid evidence for another's process (Karunarathne et al., 2022).
§2.5 · SHACL + PROV-O — never combined before

SHACL answers did the output conform to the rule? PROV-O answers where did it come from, who is responsible? Recording each SHACL validation event as a PROV-O activity yields a continuous, queryable chain — the evidentiary spine of this framework.

Committed triple
SHACL validation

Malaysia's Data Sharing Act 2025 (Act 864) maps directly: §13 permissible purpose · §15 refusal grounds → SHACL abstention shape · §21 duty to report → a SPARQL query over the provenance store.

Exhibit 03 / Methodology

Six activities, dependency-ordered, eight semesters

Design Science Research Methodology. Each objective produces and evaluates an artefact in dependency order — none is answered by observing an existing system, so DSR is a structural requirement, not a preference (Peffers et al., 2007).

ACT 1 ✓
Problem identification — thematic review, five-gap analysis. Complete.
ACT 2 ✓
Define objectives — five design objectives fixed from the literature. Complete.
ACT 3
Design & development — build A1 → A2 → A3 in dependency order.
ACT 4
Demonstration — POC prototype integrating A1–A3; two life events.
ACT 5
Evaluation — scenario testing, baselines, expert validation.
ACT 6
Communication — dissertation, three journal articles, viva.
